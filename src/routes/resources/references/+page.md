## Foundational Studies

<a aria-hidden="true" id="swan"></a>

### SWAN — Study of Women's Health Across the Nation

A multi-site longitudinal study of women through the menopausal transition. Much of what we know about symptom patterns, duration, and racial/ethnic differences comes from SWAN data.

<a aria-hidden="true" id="swan-vasomotor-duration-2015"></a>

**Avis NE, et al.** ["Duration of Menopausal Vasomotor Symptoms Over the Menopause Transition."](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2014.8063) *JAMA Internal Medicine.* 2015;175(4):531-539.
*Key finding: Median total vasomotor symptom duration was 7.4 years, with significant variation by race/ethnicity.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="swan-major-depression-2011"></a>

**Bromberger JT, et al.** ["Major Depression During and After the Menopausal Transition: Study of Women's Health Across the Nation (SWAN)."](https://doi.org/10.1017/S003329171100016X) *Psychological Medicine.* 2011;41(9):1879-1888.  [Corrigendum: 2011;41(10):2238.](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291711001620)
*Key finding: Among 221 African American and white women at SWAN's Pittsburgh site, major depression was two to four times more likely in perimenopause or early postmenopause than in premenopause.  The corrigendum corrects one confidence interval in a table, for vasomotor symptoms.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="swan-cognition-2009"></a>

**Greendale GA, et al.** ["Effects of the Menopause Transition and Hormone Use on Cognitive Performance in Midlife Women."](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0b013e3181a71193) *Neurology.* 2009;72(21):1850-1857.
*Key finding: Learning gains on cognitive tests stalled during perimenopause and rebounded postmenopause, suggesting the difficulties may be time-limited.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="swan-sleep-2008"></a>

**Kravitz HM, et al.** ["Sleep Disturbance During the Menopausal Transition in a Multi-Ethnic Community Sample of Women."](https://doi.org/10.5665/sleep/31.7.979) *Sleep.* 2008;31(7):979-990.

<a aria-hidden="true" id="transition-duration-2017"></a>

**Paramsothy P, et al.** ["Duration of the Menopausal Transition Is Longer in Women with Young Age at Onset: The Multiethnic Study of Women's Health Across the Nation."](https://doi.org/10.1097/GME.0000000000000736) *Menopause.* 2017;24(2):142-149.
*Key finding: Among 1,145 women, the adjusted median length of the transition ranged from 4.37 years in those who started it oldest to 8.57 years in those who started it youngest.  Earlier onset meant a longer transition.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="whi"></a>

### Women's Health Initiative (WHI)

The study that shaped—and complicated—our understanding of hormone therapy.

<a aria-hidden="true" id="whi-initial-2002"></a>

**Writing Group for the WHI Investigators.** ["Risks and Benefits of Estrogen Plus Progestin in Healthy Postmenopausal Women."](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.288.3.321) *JAMA.* 2002;288(3):321-333.
*The original WHI estrogen-plus-progestin findings: the trial was stopped early, with overall health risks exceeding benefits.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="whi-reanalysis-2007"></a>

**Rossouw JE, et al.** ["Postmenopausal Hormone Therapy and Risk of Cardiovascular Disease by Age and Years Since Menopause."](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.297.13.1465) *JAMA.* 2007;297(13):1465-1477.
*Secondary analysis suggesting, without reaching statistical significance, that timing affects heart disease risk; the starting point for the timing hypothesis.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="whi-estrogen-alone-2004"></a>

**WHI Steering Committee.** ["Effects of Conjugated Equine Estrogen in Postmenopausal Women with Hysterectomy."](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.291.14.1701) *JAMA.* 2004;291(14):1701-1712.
*Estrogen-alone arm showed different risk profile than combined therapy.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="whi-followup-2013"></a>

**Manson JE, et al.** ["Menopausal Hormone Therapy and Health Outcomes During the Intervention and Extended Poststopping Phases of the Women's Health Initiative Randomized Trials."](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2013.278040) *JAMA.* 2013;310(13):1353-1368.
*Long-term follow-up data.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="keeps-elite"></a>

### KEEPS and ELITE

Studies that tested the timing hypothesis for hormone therapy.

<a aria-hidden="true" id="keeps-trial-2014"></a>

**Harman SM, et al.** ["Arterial Imaging Outcomes and Cardiovascular Risk Factors in Recently Menopausal Women: A Randomized Trial."](https://doi.org/10.7326/M14-0353) *Annals of Internal Medicine.* 2014;161(4):249-260.
*Key finding: KEEPS randomized 727 recently menopausal women to oral or transdermal estrogen or placebo for four years—hormone therapy neither slowed nor accelerated atherosclerosis progression.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="keeps-imaging-2026"></a>

**Kantarci K, et al.** ["Long-Term Amyloid PET and MRI Outcomes in a Menopausal Hormone Therapy Trial."](https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71067) *Alzheimer's & Dementia.* 2026;22(2):e71067.
*Key finding: Ten years after the KEEPS trial ended, amyloid burden and brain structure did not differ between women who had taken hormone therapy and those on placebo.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="elite-2016"></a>

**Hodis HN, et al.** ["Vascular Effects of Early versus Late Postmenopausal Treatment with Estradiol."](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1505241) *New England Journal of Medicine.* 2016;374(13):1221-1231.
*Key finding: Started within 6 years of menopause, oral estradiol slowed carotid intima-media thickening compared with placebo; started 10+ years after, it did not.  No significant effect on coronary CT measures in either group.*

## Hormonal Landscape & Physiology

<a aria-hidden="true" id="straw-2012"></a>

**Harlow SD, et al.** ["Executive Summary of the Stages of Reproductive Aging Workshop +10: Addressing the Unfinished Agenda of Staging Reproductive Aging."](https://doi.org/10.1097/gme.0b013e31824d8f40) *Menopause.* 2012;19(4):387-395.
*The STRAW+10 staging system that defines perimenopause.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="prior-perimenopause-1998"></a>

**Prior JC.** ["Perimenopause: The Complex Endocrinology of the Menopausal Transition."](https://doi.org/10.1210/edrv.19.4.0341) *Endocrine Reviews.* 1998;19(4):397-428.
*Key finding: As the author later summarized it (Prior 2011), one meta-analysis found follicular-phase estradiol averaged roughly 25-30% higher in perimenopausal women than in premenopausal women.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="prior-estradiol-2011"></a>

**Prior JC.** ["Progesterone for Symptomatic Perimenopause Treatment – Progesterone Politics, Physiology and Potential for Perimenopause."](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3987489/) *Facts, Views & Vision in ObGyn.* 2011;3(2):109-120.
*Key finding: Describes a meta-analysis of within-centre comparisons: follicular-phase estradiol of 225 pmol/L in perimenopausal women (n=415) versus 175 pmol/L in premenopausal women (n=290).  This is the author's position, and the paper also quotes a cohort (Burger) that concluded the opposite.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="santoro-perimenopause-2016"></a>

**Santoro N.** ["Perimenopause: From Research to Practice."](https://doi.org/10.1089/jwh.2015.5556) *Journal of Women's Health.* 2016;25(4):332-339.

## Hot Flashes & Vasomotor Symptoms

<a aria-hidden="true" id="kndy-thermoregulation-2013"></a>

**Rance NE, et al.** ["Modulation of Body Temperature and LH Secretion by Hypothalamic KNDy (Kisspeptin, Neurokinin B and Dynorphin) Neurons: A Novel Hypothesis on the Mechanism of Hot Flushes."](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yfrne.2013.07.003) *Frontiers in Neuroendocrinology.* 2013;34(3):211-227.
*Review proposing that KNDy neurons relay estrogen signals to thermoregulatory areas and help generate hot flashes, based largely on rat and postmortem human data.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="kndy-circuit-mouse-2018"></a>

**Padilla SL, et al.** ["A Neural Circuit Underlying the Generation of Hot Flushes."](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2018.06.037) *Cell Reports.* 2018;24(2):271-277.
*Key finding: A mouse study, not a human one.  Transient activation of hypothalamic Kiss1 neurons after sex-hormone withdrawal contributed to hot flushes through neurokinin B release in the rostral preoptic area.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="vms-management-review-2023"></a>

**Crandall CJ, Mehta JM, Manson JE.** ["Management of Menopausal Symptoms: A Review."](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.24140) *JAMA.* 2023;329(5):405-420.
*Key finding: During the menopausal transition, approximately 50% to 75% of women have hot flashes, night sweats, or both.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="vms-impact-survey"></a>

**DePree B, et al.** ["Association of Menopausal Vasomotor Symptom Severity with Sleep and Work Impairments: A US Survey."](https://doi.org/10.1097/GME.0000000000002237) *Menopause.* 2023;30(9):887-897.
*Key finding: In an Astellas-sponsored online survey of 619 US women with at least 14 hot flashes or night sweats a week, 90.8% reported that symptoms affect sleep (97.7% with severe symptoms), and 83.1% of those reported sleep-related changes in productivity.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="vms-cvd-risk"></a>

**Zhu D, et al.** ["Vasomotor Menopausal Symptoms and Risk of Cardiovascular Disease: A Pooled Analysis of Six Prospective Studies."](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajog.2020.06.039) *American Journal of Obstetrics & Gynecology.* 2020;223(6):898.e1-898.e16.
*Key finding: Severe hot flashes associated with 83% higher CVD risk; night sweats with 59% higher risk. VMS may serve as markers for cardiovascular risk.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="vms-subclinical-cvd"></a>

**Thurston RC, et al.** ["Hot Flashes and Subclinical Cardiovascular Disease: Findings from the Study of Women's Health Across the Nation Heart Study."](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.108.776823) *Circulation.* 2008;118(12):1234-1240.
*Key finding: Women with hot flashes had evidence of poorer endothelial function and higher aortic calcification, suggesting hot flashes may be markers of adverse vascular changes.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="mood"></a>

## Mood & Mental Health

<a aria-hidden="true" id="depression-odds-meta-2024"></a>

**Badawy Y, et al.** ["The Risk of Depression in the Menopausal Stages: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis."](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2024.04.041) *Journal of Affective Disorders.* 2024;357:126-133.
*Key finding: Perimenopausal women had 40% higher odds of depressive symptoms or diagnosis than premenopausal women (OR 1.40; 95% CI 1.21-1.61).  No significant increase after menopause.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="weber-mood-meta-2014"></a>

**Weber MT, et al.** ["Cognition and Mood in Perimenopause: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis."](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsbmb.2013.06.001) *Journal of Steroid Biochemistry and Molecular Biology.* 2014;142:90-98.
*Key finding: Meta-analysis found elevated depression risk in perimenopausal and postmenopausal women compared with premenopausal women; verbal-memory differences emerged after menopause, not during the transition.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="anxiety-perimenopause"></a>

**Bromberger JT, Epperson CN.** ["Depression During and After the Perimenopause: Impact of Hormones, Genetics, and Environmental Determinants of Disease."](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ogc.2018.07.007) *Obstetrics and Gynecology Clinics of North America.* 2018;45(4):663-678.

<a aria-hidden="true" id="ht-depression-meta-2026"></a>

**Li Y, et al.** ["Efficacy and Safety of Menopausal Hormone Therapy for Depressive Symptoms in Perimenopausal Women: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis."](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2026.121892) *Journal of Affective Disorders.* 2026;409:121892.
*Key finding: Twelve randomized trials reviewed; in placebo-controlled comparisons, hormone therapy was associated with a small reduction in depressive symptoms (SMD −0.23, 95% CI −0.43 to −0.03), with limited certainty of evidence.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="bipolar-menopause-review"></a>

**Perich T, et al.** ["Menopause and Illness Course in Bipolar Disorder: A Systematic Review."](https://doi.org/10.1111/bdi.12530) *Bipolar Disorders.* 2017;19(6):434-443.

<a aria-hidden="true" id="bipolar-first-onset"></a>

**Shitomi-Jones LM, et al.** ["Exploration of First Onsets of Mania, Schizophrenia Spectrum Disorders and Major Depressive Disorder in Perimenopause."](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44220-024-00292-4) *Nature Mental Health.* 2024;2(10):1161-1168.
*Key finding: In UK Biobank (128,294 women), first onsets of mania/bipolar disorder were about twice as frequent in perimenopause as 6–10 years before the final menstrual period (RR 2.12, 95% CI 1.30–3.52; 55 vs 26 cases), returning to that level after menopause.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="cognition"></a>

## Cognitive Changes

<a aria-hidden="true" id="brain-fog-real"></a>

**Maki PM, Henderson VW.** ["Cognition and the Menopause Transition."](https://doi.org/10.1097/GME.0000000000000681) *Menopause.* 2016;23(7):803-805.

<a aria-hidden="true" id="ht-dementia-meta-2025"></a>

**Melville M, et al.** ["Menopause Hormone Therapy and Risk of Mild Cognitive Impairment or Dementia: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis."](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanhl.2025.100803) *The Lancet Healthy Longevity.* 2025;6(12):100803.
*Key finding: Ten studies (one RCT, nine observational), over one million participants: no significant association between hormone therapy and dementia or MCI in either direction; certainty moderate to very low.  WHO 2026 advises against HT for dementia prevention at 65+ and makes no recommendation under 65.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="cognition-perimenopause-meta"></a>

**Bangle A, et al.** ["Cognitive Functioning in Perimenopause: An Updated Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis."](https://doi.org/10.1037/pag0000946) *Psychology and Aging.* 2026;41(3):303-318.
*Key finding: 26 articles, 9,428 participants.  Perimenopausal women had poorer overall cognitive outcomes than premenopausal women (moderate effect, g = −0.51), pooled across objective and self-reported measures and significant only in studies using STRAW+10 staging.  No specific cognitive domain differed significantly, and there was no overall difference from postmenopausal women.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="cognition-menopause-cohort-2026"></a>

**Naysmith LF, et al.** ["Cognition and the menopause transition: cross-sectional evidence from a large community cohort."](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44294-026-00132-z) *npj Women's Health.* 2026;4:14.
*Key finding: In 14,234 women aged 45–55, perimenopausal participants reported more cognitive symptoms (brain fog OR=1.31, poor memory OR=1.28) but scored marginally higher on objective cognitive tests than pre- and postmenopausal participants.  Cognitive symptoms were weakly associated with objective performance but moderately associated with psychological symptoms.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="sleep"></a>

## Sleep

<a aria-hidden="true" id="sleep-40-percent"></a>

**Baker FC, et al.** ["Sleep Problems During the Menopausal Transition: Prevalence, Impact, and Management Challenges."](https://doi.org/10.2147/NSS.S125807) *Nature and Science of Sleep.* 2018;10:73-95.
*Key finding: Self-reported sleep difficulty runs 40–56% across the menopausal transition and postmenopause versus 31% in late-reproductive women; 26% of perimenopausal women in one survey met criteria for insomnia.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="sleep-apnea-risk"></a>

**Mirer AG, et al.** ["Sleep-Disordered Breathing and the Menopausal Transition Among Participants in the Sleep in Midlife Women Study."](https://doi.org/10.1097/GME.0000000000000744) *Menopause.* 2017;24(2):157-162.

<a aria-hidden="true" id="sleep-apnea-estrogen"></a>

**Shahar E, et al.** ["Hormone Replacement Therapy and Sleep-Disordered Breathing."](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.200210-1238OC) *American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine.* 2003;167(9):1186-1192.

<a aria-hidden="true" id="sleep-apnea-ht-bias-2015"></a>

**Mirer AG, et al.** ["Menopausal Hormone Therapy and Sleep-Disordered Breathing: Evidence for a Healthy User Bias."](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annepidem.2015.07.004) *Annals of Epidemiology.* 2015;25(10):779-784.

<a aria-hidden="true" id="sleep-apnea-menopause-prevalence"></a>

**Young T, et al.** ["Menopausal Status and Sleep-Disordered Breathing in the Wisconsin Sleep Cohort Study."](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.200209-1055OC) *American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine.* 2003;167(9):1181-1185.
*Key finding: Adjusted odds of sleep-disordered breathing (AHI ≥5) were 2.6 times higher in postmenopause (95% CI 1.4–4.8) and 3.5 times higher at AHI ≥15 (1.4–8.8), compared with premenopause; perimenopause was not significantly different.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="sleep-apnea-women-2001"></a>

**Bixler EO, et al.** ["Prevalence of Sleep-Disordered Breathing in Women: Effects of Gender."](https://doi.org/10.1164/ajrccm.163.3.9911064) *American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine.* 2001;163(3 Pt 1):608-613.
*Key finding: Clinically defined sleep apnea affected 0.6% of premenopausal women, 0.5% of postmenopausal women on hormone therapy, 2.7% of postmenopausal women not on it, and 3.9% of men.  The postmenopausal rate stayed significantly below men's after controlling for age and BMI.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="sleep-apnea-aha-cardiovascular"></a>

**Makarem N, et al.** ["Association of Sleep Characteristics with Cardiovascular Health Among Women and Differences by Race/Ethnicity and Menopausal Status: Findings from the American Heart Association Go Red for Women Strategically Focused Research Network."](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sleh.2019.05.005) *Sleep Health.* 2019;5(5):501-508.

<a aria-hidden="true" id="cbti-meta-analysis-2015"></a>

**Trauer JM, et al.** ["Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Chronic Insomnia: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis."](https://doi.org/10.7326/M14-2841) *Annals of Internal Medicine.* 2015;163(3):191-204.
*Key finding: CBT-I produces clinically meaningful improvements in sleep onset latency, wake after sleep onset, and sleep efficiency, with effects that seemed to be sustained at later time points, though those later estimates were less certain.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="cbti-vs-medication-2012"></a>

**Mitchell MD, et al.** ["Comparative Effectiveness of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia: A Systematic Review."](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2296-13-40) *BMC Family Practice.* 2012;13:40.
*Key finding: Five trials compared CBT-I with medication.  Low to moderate grade evidence suggests it outperforms benzodiazepine and non-benzodiazepine drugs in the long term, while very low grade evidence suggests benzodiazepines work better in the short term.  Not menopause-specific.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="mindfulness-sleep-nma-2025"></a>

**Wang Z, et al.** ["Effectiveness of nonpharmacological interventions for menopause-related insomnia: A systematic review and Bayesian network meta-analysis."](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.maturitas.2025.108713) *Maturitas.* 2025;202:108713.
*Key finding: Mindfulness ranked as the most effective nonpharmacological intervention for menopausal insomnia (MD: −5.61) across 44 trials, ahead of CBT, relaxation, acupuncture, aromatherapy, massage, yoga, and exercise — all eight interventions significantly outperformed control.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="progesterone-sleep-2008"></a>

**Schüssler P, et al.** ["Progesterone Reduces Wakefulness in Sleep EEG and Has No Effect on Cognition in Healthy Postmenopausal Women."](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psyneuen.2008.05.013) *Psychoneuroendocrinology.* 2008;33(8):1124-1131.
*Key finding: Progesterone demonstrated a distinct sleep-promoting effect by reducing time awake without impairing daytime cognition. A GABA-agonistic effect is discussed as one possible mechanism.*

## Body Composition & Weight

<a aria-hidden="true" id="visceral-fat-shift"></a>

**Lovejoy JC, et al.** ["Increased Visceral Fat and Decreased Energy Expenditure During the Menopausal Transition."](https://doi.org/10.1038/ijo.2008.25) *International Journal of Obesity.* 2008;32(6):949-958.

<a aria-hidden="true" id="fat-redistribution"></a>

**Greendale GA, et al.** ["Changes in Body Composition and Weight During the Menopause Transition."](https://doi.org/10.1172/jci.insight.124865) *JCI Insight.* 2019;4(5):e124865.
*Key finding: In SWAN, fat gain doubled and lean mass began to decline at the start of the menopause transition, with no change in the rate of weight gain.  Regional and visceral fat were not assessed.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="glp1-exercise-2021"></a>

**Lundgren JR, et al.** ["Healthy Weight Loss Maintenance with Exercise, Liraglutide, or Both Combined."](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2028198) *New England Journal of Medicine.* 2021;384(18):1719-1730.

<a aria-hidden="true" id="semaglutide-ht-2024"></a>

**Hurtado MD, et al.** ["Weight Loss Response to Semaglutide in Postmenopausal Women With and Without Hormone Therapy Use."](https://doi.org/10.1097/GME.0000000000002310) *Menopause.* 2024;31(4):266-274.

<a aria-hidden="true" id="tirzepatide-surmount-menopause"></a>

**Tchang BG, et al.** ["Body Weight Reduction in Women Treated with Tirzepatide by Reproductive Stage: A Post Hoc Analysis from the SURMOUNT Program."](https://doi.org/10.1002/oby.24254) *Obesity (Silver Spring).* 2025;33(5):851-860.
*Key finding: In SURMOUNT-1, tirzepatide produced roughly 23-26% weight reduction in pre-, peri-, and postmenopausal women alike (post hoc analysis; manufacturer-funded).*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="tirzepatide-ht-2026"></a>

**Castaneda R, et al.** ["The Role of Menopause Hormone Therapy in Modulating Tirzepatide-Associated Weight Loss in Postmenopausal Women with Overweight or Obesity: A Retrospective Cohort Study."](https://doi.org/10.1016/S3050-5038(25)00145-1) *The Lancet Obstetrics, Gynaecology, & Women's Health.* 2026;2(2):e118-e128.
*Key finding: In a Mayo Clinic cohort of 120 postmenopausal women on tirzepatide for at least a year, hormone therapy users lost roughly 35% more weight (19.2% vs 14.0% of body weight).  Retrospective—not randomized.*

## Pelvic Floor & Urogenital

<a aria-hidden="true" id="incontinence-prevalence"></a>

**Nygaard I, et al.** ["Prevalence of Symptomatic Pelvic Floor Disorders in US Women."](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.300.11.1311) *JAMA.* 2008;300(11):1311-1316.
*Key finding: Nearly 1 in 4 US women have symptoms of a pelvic floor disorder; prevalence rises with age, from 9.7% at 20-39 to 49.7% at 80 and older.  Menopausal status was not analyzed.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="viva-survey-2012"></a>

**Nappi RE, Kokot-Kierepa M.** ["Vaginal Health: Insights, Views & Attitudes (VIVA) – Results from an International Survey."](https://doi.org/10.3109/13697137.2011.647840) *Climacteric.* 2012;15(1):36-44.
*Key finding: 45% of postmenopausal women reported experiencing vaginal symptoms.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="gsm-review-2015"></a>

**Kim HK, et al.** ["The Recent Review of the Genitourinary Syndrome of Menopause."](https://doi.org/10.6118/jmm.2015.21.2.65) *Journal of Menopausal Medicine.* 2015;21(2):65-71.
*Key finding: Narrative review.  Unlike vasomotor symptoms, which often improve over time, genitourinary symptoms are chronic, rarely resolve on their own, and often progress if left untreated.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="prolapse-prevalence"></a>

**Wu JM, et al.** ["Prevalence and Trends of Symptomatic Pelvic Floor Disorders in U.S. Women."](https://doi.org/10.1097/AOG.0000000000000057) *Obstetrics & Gynecology.* 2014;123(1):141-148.

<a aria-hidden="true" id="vaginal-estrogen-safety"></a>

**ACOG Committee Opinion No. 659.** ["The Use of Vaginal Estrogen in Women with a History of Estrogen-Dependent Breast Cancer."](https://doi.org/10.1097/AOG.0000000000001351) *Obstetrics & Gynecology.* 2016;127(3):e93-e96.

<a aria-hidden="true" id="skin"></a>

## Skin Changes

<a aria-hidden="true" id="brincat-collagen-1987"></a>

**Brincat M, et al.** ["A Study of the Decrease of Skin Collagen Content, Skin Thickness, and Bone Mass in the Postmenopausal Woman."](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3120067/) *Obstetrics & Gynecology.* 1987;70(6):840-845.
*Key finding: Skin collagen content, skin thickness, metacarpal index and forearm bone mineral content each declined by 1-2% per year after menopause, and the four measures correlated with one another.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="thornton-skin-2013"></a>

**Thornton MJ.** ["Estrogens and Aging Skin."](https://doi.org/10.4161/derm.23872) *Dermato-Endocrinology.* 2013;5(2):264-270.
*Key finding: Review stating that type I and III skin collagen "is thought to decrease by as much as 30% in the first five years after menopause", alongside a figure of 2% per postmenopausal year that it doesn't reconcile with the first.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="skin-estrogen-biology-2006"></a>

**Verdier-Sévrain S, Bonté F, Gilchrest BA.** ["Biology of estrogens in skin: implications for skin aging."](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0625.2005.00377.x) *Experimental Dermatology.* 2006;15(2):83-94.
*Key finding: Estrogen receptors (ER-α and ER-β) are distributed throughout skin — in the epidermis, dermis, sebaceous glands, hair follicles, and mast cells. Estrogen regulates dermal hyaluronic acid synthesis by fibroblasts, and its decline may alter skin hydration, sensitivity, and wound healing capacity.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="fphl-pathophysiology-2018"></a>

**Fabbrocini G, et al.** ["Female pattern hair loss: A clinical, pathophysiologic, and therapeutic review."](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijwd.2018.05.001) *International Journal of Women's Dermatology.* 2018;4(4):203-211.
*Key finding: Female pattern hair loss involves progressive follicular miniaturization, typically as diffuse thinning with the frontal hairline preserved.  Genetic predisposition and androgens play a part in some women, but the role of androgens is less clear than in men, and it can develop without them.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="zouboulis-menopause-skin-2022"></a>

**Zouboulis CC, Blume-Peytavi U, Kosmadaki M, et al.** ["Skin, hair and beyond: the impact of menopause."](https://doi.org/10.1080/13697137.2022.2050206) *Climacteric.* 2022;25(5):434-442.
*Key finding: Review of menopause-related skin and hair symptoms, including dryness, pruritus, thinning, atrophy, poor wound healing, reduced vascularity and hair loss.  These often receive less attention than other menopausal symptoms despite a significant effect on quality of life.  Skin aging signs and skin cancers are attributed almost exclusively to environmental factors, especially sun.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="fphl-treatment-update-2023"></a>

**Müller Ramos P, Melo DF, Radwanski H, de Almeida RFC, Miot HA.** ["Female-pattern hair loss: therapeutic update."](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.abd.2022.09.006) *Anais Brasileiros de Dermatologia.* 2023;98(4):506-519.
*Key finding: Topical minoxidil remains the best-supported first-line treatment for female pattern hair loss. Low-level laser therapy, oral minoxidil, and other approaches have evidence as adjuncts or alternatives.*

<a aria-hidden="true" id="lllt-pattern-hair-loss-meta-2021"></a>

**Lueangarun S, Visutjindaporn P, Parcharoen Y, Jamparuang P, Tempark T.** ["A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials of United States Food and Drug Administration-Approved, Home-use, Low-Level Light/Laser Therapy Devices for Pattern Hair Loss: Device Design and Technology."](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8675345/) *Journal of Clinical and Aesthetic Dermatology.* 2021;14(11):E64-E75.

<a aria-hidden="true" id="red-clover-meta"></a>

**Kanadys W, et al.** ["Evaluation of Clinical Meaningfulness of Red Clover (Trifolium pratense L.) <a aria-hidden="true" id="sage-meta"></a>

**Moradi M, et al.** ["The Effect of Salvia Officinalis on Hot Flashes in Postmenopausal Women: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis."](https://doi.org/10.30476/IJCBNM.2023.97639.2198) *International Journal of Community Based Nursing and Midwifery.* 2023;11(3):169-178.

<a aria-hidden="true" id="ashwagandha-meta"></a>

**Akhgarjand C, et al.** ["Does Ashwagandha Supplementation Have a Beneficial Effect on the Management of Anxiety and Stress? A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials."](https://doi.org/10.1002/ptr.7598) *Phytotherapy Research.* 2022;36(11):4115-4124.

<a aria-hidden="true" id="bacopa-meta"></a>

**Kongkeaw C, et al.** ["Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials on Cognitive Effects of Bacopa monnieri Extract."](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2013.11.008) *Journal of Ethnopharmacology.* 2014;151(1):528-535.

<a aria-hidden="true" id="hops-rct"></a>

**Heyerick A, et al.** ["A First Prospective, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study on the Use of a Standardized Hop Extract to Alleviate Menopausal Discomforts."](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.maturitas.2005.10.005) *Maturitas.* 2006;54(2):164-175.

<a aria-hidden="true" id="shatavari-rct"></a>

**Mahajan S, et al.** ["Efficacy and Safety of Shatavari (Asparagus racemosus) Root Extract for Perimenopause: Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study."](https://doi.org/10.2147/IJWH.S544267) *International Journal of Women's Health.* 2025;17:4057-4073.
